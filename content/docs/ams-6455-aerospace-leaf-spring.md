title: "AMS 6455 and Aerospace Leaf Spring Manufacturing"
date: 2019-07-08
layout: "single"
---
AMS 6455 is the SAE Aerospace Material Specification for 6150 chromium-vanadium alloy steel in sheet and strip form, covering thicknesses from 0.010 inch to 0.375 inch in the annealed, normalized, or quenched and tempered condition. While the specification number suggests a material standard, AMS 6455 is in practice a manufacturing and quality framework for aerospace leaf springs, Belleville disc springs, and formed torsion members where the fatigue resistance and hardenability of 6150 bar are required in a flat-rolled form suitable for blanking, forming, and heat treatment.

The primary difference between AMS 6455 and a general-purpose spring steel specification is the level of control over surface quality and flatness. Aerospace leaf springs are fatigue-critical components: a surface defect, a decarburized layer, or a flatness deviation that introduces an unintended bending moment can reduce fatigue life by an order of magnitude. AMS 6455 therefore imposes tight flatness requirements and surface quality acceptance criteria that are not typically found in industrial spring steel specifications. The annealed sheet is supplied at a maximum hardness of 229 HBW to facilitate blanking and forming; after forming, the parts are heat treated to a tensile strength of 190,000–200,000 psi, with a minimum elongation of 10% to survive the deflection strains of the spring without fracturing.

Shot peening is not optional in the AMS 6455 process chain. After heat treatment, aerospace leaf springs are shot peened in accordance with MIL-S-13165 or AMS 2430. The purpose is to introduce compressive residual stresses of 100,000–150,000 psi at the surface, which directly offset the applied tensile fatigue stresses and extend fatigue life by a factor of 3 to 5. This is not a cosmetic treatment; it is a structural requirement that compensates for the fact that high-strength steel, while strong, is sensitive to surface-initiated fatigue when subjected to the bending stresses of a leaf spring. A leaf spring that meets the chemical and tensile requirements of AMS 6455 but has not been properly shot peened will not deliver the fatigue life that the aerospace application demands.

The certification requirements under AMS 6455 are correspondingly detailed. The material certification must include chemical composition per heat, including vanadium content; mechanical properties per lot in the applicable condition; flatness and surface quality inspection results; and dimensional certification. Spring manufacturers may additionally specify bend test results, surface roughness limits, and decarburization depth limits per the applicable aerospace spring standard. Material is traceable by heat and lot number throughout the manufacturing process.

For a spring manufacturer entering the aerospace supply chain, AMS 6455 illustrates a broader principle: aerospace spring specifications are not merely material grades. They define a process window that includes forming, heat treatment, shot peening, and inspection, and compliance is demonstrated through a chain of certifications rather than a single test report. The leaf spring is a useful case study because it combines high bending stresses, fatigue-critical surfaces, and a geometry that is sensitive to flatness and residual stress—three factors that AMS 6455 addresses explicitly.
